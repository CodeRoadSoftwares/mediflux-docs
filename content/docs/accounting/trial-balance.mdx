A **Trial Balance** lists every ledger account in your Chart of Accounts along with its opening balance, total debits, total credits, and closing balance for a given period. It is the checkpoint that tells you whether your books are mathematically correct. If total debits equal total credits, your double-entry bookkeeping is in balance.

In MediFlux, the Trial Balance is generated automatically from all posted journal entries. You do not need to calculate anything manually.

Navigate here from the sidebar: **Accounting → Trial Balance**.

## Total Debits and Total Credits

Two large cards sit below the category cards:

| Card | What it shows |
|------|---------------|
| **Total Debits** | The sum of all debit columns across every account in the selected period |
| **Total Credits** | The sum of all credit columns across every account in the selected period |

These two numbers must be **exactly equal**. If they match, the trial balance tallies. If they do not match, there is a bookkeeping error that must be resolved.

## Accounts table

The main table lists every parent account group. Click the arrow next to any parent to expand it and see its child accounts.

| Column | What it means |
|--------|---------------|
| **Ledger** | Account name with `Dr` or `Cr` badge. Parent groups can be expanded to reveal children |
| **Type** | Color-coded category tag such as `CURRENT ASSET`, `CURRENT LIABILITY`, `EQUITY`, `REVENUE`, or `EXPENSE` |
| **Opening** | The account balance at the start of the selected period |
| **Debit** | Total amount debited to this account during the period |
| **Credit** | Total amount credited to this account during the period |
| **Closing** | The account balance at the end of the period |

### How the closing balance is calculated

| Normal balance | Formula |
|----------------|---------|
| **Debit Normal** (Assets, Expenses) | `Closing = Opening + Debit − Credit` |
| **Credit Normal** (Liabilities, Equity, Revenue) | `Closing = Opening + Credit − Debit` |

## Why the trial balance must tally

Every transaction affects at least two accounts, and the total debits always equal the total credits in every journal entry. When you add up all debits and all credits across every account for the period, the two sides must still be equal.

| Principle | Explanation |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Every transaction is balanced** | A sale debits Cash or Accounts Receivable and credits Revenue. A purchase debits Inventory and credits Cash or Accounts Payable. The two sides always match |
| **Contra accounts preserve the balance** | When a sale is returned, Revenue is debited and Cash or Accounts Receivable is credited. Both sides still match |
| **The total row proves correctness** | If `Total Debits = Total Credits`, every journal entry was posted correctly |

### What if the totals do not match?

| Error | What to check |
|-------|---------------|
| **Missing journal entry** | A transaction was recorded in one account but not the other |
| **Wrong amount posted** | The debit and credit sides of a journal entry were entered with different amounts |
| **Entry posted twice** | The same journal entry was posted twice |
| **Account not initialized** | A ledger was used in a transaction but was not properly set up in the Chart of Accounts |

<Note>
MediFlux prevents most of these errors at the source. When you create a journal entry, the system requires that total debits equal total credits before the entry can be saved.
</Note>

## Common mistakes

<Warning title="Panicking when a category shows both debit and credit">
A category like **Current Asset** showing both green (debit) and red (credit) does not mean your books are wrong. Contra-assets such as **Purchase Return** and **Accumulated Depreciation** naturally carry credit balances. Expand the parent group to see which accounts are causing it.
</Warning>

<Warning title="Comparing Trial Balance directly to your bank statement">
The Trial Balance includes non-cash accounts like **Accounts Receivable** and **Accounts Payable**. It is not a cash report. To reconcile your bank balance, open the **Cash & Bank** ledger or use the **Balance Sheet**.
</Warning>

## Frequently asked questions

<Accordions>
  <Accordion title="What is the difference between Trial Balance and Balance Sheet?">
    The **Trial Balance** lists every ledger account with its activity and balance. It is an internal tool used to verify that debits equal credits. The **Balance Sheet** is a financial statement that groups accounts into Assets, Liabilities, and Equity to show the financial position of the business at a point in time.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Why does the Current Asset card show a credit amount?">
    This happens because of **contra-asset** accounts like **Purchase Return** or **Accumulated Depreciation**, which carry credit balances. These credits reduce the net value of your assets.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can I export the Trial Balance?">
    Yes. Click **Export** and choose either **PDF Document** for a formatted report or **Excel Sheet** for further analysis.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What period should I select?">
    Most businesses run the Trial Balance monthly. You can also run it for a quarter, half-year, or full financial year by adjusting the date range or selecting a different fiscal year.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Does a balanced Trial Balance mean there are no errors?">
    A balanced Trial Balance confirms that total debits equal total credits. It does not catch errors of omission (a transaction was completely forgotten) or errors of principle (a transaction was posted to the wrong type of account).
  </Accordion>
</Accordions>
